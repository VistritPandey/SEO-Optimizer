# Strategy, research and content

Checks: content.strategy, content.competitors, content.factual, content.originality, content.refresh. Sources: google-helpful, google-starter, google-spam, google-ai, google-canonical in the [registry](../sources.json).

## Strategy inputs and prioritization

Start with the business goal, audience, supported products/services and conversion path. Map a query or topic's intent to an existing or proposed useful landing page. Treat search-volume numbers as unknown unless supplied by an identified data source with date and market. Do not invent difficulty scores, demand, traffic projections or competitors.

Research competitors using dated attributable observations. Determine what question is underserved, what the business can credibly contribute and whether existing content already answers it. More pages, more words or more backlinks in a competitor report do not establish the best next action. Rank opportunities by goal relevance, demonstrated impact, confidence and effort; explain tradeoffs rather than fabricating an impact formula.

## Brief and draft requirements

A content brief identifies audience/task, intended page, existing coverage, distinctive contribution, required facts, primary evidence, internal links and success metric. Use firsthand records, genuine product information, original analysis or credible sourced explanations. Do not fabricate experience, author qualifications, research results, review quotes or customer stories.

Write readable sections at the depth the task needs. There is no universal minimum length, keyword density, fixed heading count or mandatory exact-match phrasing. Tables and direct answers can help when they clarify content; do not force every page into the same template. AI assistance is acceptable when the result is accurate and useful; creating large quantities primarily to manipulate visibility is not.

Every material factual claim needs a source appropriate to its specificity and time. Trusted project facts govern business assertions; web research supports broader statements only when the source actually supports them. Conflicting or expired facts become dependencies. The current harness validates referenced fact IDs, not the truth of every sentence, so production publication also requires claim-level checking.

## Refresh and consolidation

Update a page because a fact, intent or user need changed, not solely because its publication date is old. Preserve still-correct content. Diagnose whether overlap is redundant or serves distinct audiences/tasks before merging pages. A consolidation requires an equivalent destination, redirects, navigation updates and verification of retained coverage. Do not delete pages based only on short content or temporarily low traffic.

Automatic publication needs project permission, factual support, a real original contribution, editorial checks and successful runtime verification. If those are absent, record the missing inputs. Content quality and search outcomes remain partly judgment-based and cannot be certified by a single model self-rating.
